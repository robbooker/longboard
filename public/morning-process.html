● Order of operations

The morning sequence.

This is not a theory page. It is the order of operations: state, scan, reset, prepare, execute — then back to the short side, only after the long trade is managed.

  1. 01State
    6:30 AM CT

    Check yourself before the market.

    Start with the question that matters: am I at my best, or at least in a trading state that is calm enough to follow instructions?

    For Rob, the key input is not sleep, food, or exercise. It is emotional peace with the people around him. Your list may be different. The point is to know the list before the market starts offering distractions.

    Anchor phrase

    Coffee. Pool. Quiet. “I'm ready.” Say it only when it is true.

  2. 02Shorts
    7:00–8:00 AM CT Short side

    Get the shorting out of your system.

    Open every source of stocks moving up. Use several gainers lists, because no scanner catches everything. During this window, the only job is short-side thinking.

    • No guru changes the window.
    • No authority talks you into buying early.
    • Pick the window that works for you and protect it.
  3. 03Reset
    8:00 AM CT

    Walk away for ten minutes.

    When the short window ends, leave the desk. Read the paper, move around, splash cold water on your face — anything that breaks the stare-at-screen loop.

    The reset matters because the next window is the one most likely to pay, and the worst version of you is the one that has been leaning into the monitor too long.

  4. 04Prep
    8:20 CT / 9:20 ET

    Do your future self a favor.

    Back at the desk, reduce the scanner to the names closest to their pre-market highs. That is the filter. Not the news, not the float, not the story.

    • Load the top four candidates.
    • Set share size before the open.
    • Keep montage windows ready.
    • Close the chat rooms at the open.
    Longboard advantage

    Missed pivot on the chart, gainers on the side, RVOL signals on the side, fundamentals in reach, target already visible. One screen should carry the trade.

  5. 05Open
    8:30 CT / 9:30 ET

    Take the five-minute trade.

    The highest-value long trades usually fire between 9:30 and 9:45 Eastern, when volume rushes in and the system has something real to measure.

    • Entry: five-minute signal.
    • Target: the missed pivot.
    • Stop: under the signal candle, roughly 5–10% below.
    • Order: range order in immediately.
    Execution bias

    Do not overthink it. Underthink it. Range order in, hands off.

  6. 06Manage
    After the open Short side

    Return to the short side.

    Once the long trade has its range order, there is nothing useful to do by hovering. Go back to managing shorts, where adding to winners and moving stops takes more active attention.

    Do not short names already ruled out as short candidates, and do not short anything you are actively long in a Longboard trade. When a Longboard trade reaches its missed pivot, it can become a new short idea. Not before.

● The whole thing

The process in nine lines.

Short enough to copy. Specific enough to matter.

  1. Check in with yourself before you check the market.
  2. Start the day with something besides the market.
  3. Say “I'm ready” only when it is true.
  4. Not trading today is a valid trade.
  5. Pick your windows, and let no guru move them.
  6. Do as many favors for your future self as you can.
  7. Volume moves stocks. Float does not.
  8. Do not overthink the trade. Underthink it.
  9. Range order in, hands off, walk away.
